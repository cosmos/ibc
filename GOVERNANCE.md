# Governance

IBC operates under a Technical Steering Committee (TSC) defined in the Technical Charter. Governance is lightweight by design: decisions run on lazy consensus, with a vote as the fallback. The TSC oversees the technical direction of the project, and day-to-day authority sits with the Maintainers.

## Roles

The Charter defines two roles; their criteria are documented here.

- **Contributor:** Anyone who contributes code, specs, documentation, or other artifacts, for example by opening an issue, discussion, or pull request. Open to all.
- **Maintainer:** A Contributor who has earned the ability to merge, granted by majority approval of the TSC after a sustained record of substantial contributions. As a guideline, a contributor is typically considered after a sustained period of substantial, reviewed contributions to the project, on the order of a few months of consistent activity, rather than a fixed count of pull requests. The TSC sets and applies the bar. Maintainers hold merge rights across the repository, and the TSC may introduce area-based scoping later if needed. Maintainers are listed in [MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md). Maintainers are the voting members of the TSC.

Merge authority is earned through the same public record for everyone.

## Technical Steering Committee

The TSC is the body of Maintainers. Every Maintainer is a voting member with one vote. The TSC decides by consensus where it can and by vote where it must, with a quorum of at least half the voting members, a majority of those present at a meeting, or a majority of all voting members for an electronic vote. The TSC elects a Chair, who presides over meetings and is the primary contact with LFDT. The initial Maintainers are the initial TSC and are listed in the CONTRIBUTING file, as the Charter requires. A Contributor becomes a Maintainer, and so a voting member, by majority approval of the TSC. If the maintainer set grows large, the TSC may adopt an alternative approach to voting membership, documented in the CONTRIBUTING file, as the Charter allows.

## Decision-making

Lazy consensus is the default: a change proceeds unless someone with standing objects within the stated window, see CONTRIBUTING.md. Where consensus cannot be reached, the TSC votes. A change that affects a specification routes through the ADR process. Maintainers and the TSC decide what to take up and when.
